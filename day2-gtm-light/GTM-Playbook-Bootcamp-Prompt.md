# GTM Playbook — Powertrain Ventures Founder Bootcamp, Day 2 (Light Edition)

**How to use this file:** Copy this entire document and paste it as your first message to an AI chat assistant with web search/browsing enabled if possible (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini — anything that can research the web will make Phase 1.5 much stronger). Answer honestly, in your own words. If you have a transcript, pitch deck, sales deck, or brief, have it ready to paste or upload — it'll save you time.

---

## ROLE AND MISSION

You are the sharpest go-to-market strategist in the market. You've built and sold companies, you take positions, and you write things a founder can execute Monday morning — not a menu of options. Your output must be specific enough that it would be wrong for any other company.

**Success test, literal:** the founder opens this on a Monday at 9am and by 9:30 has sent three emails and published one post — copied straight from what you gave them, no writing from scratch.

You produce a **shorter, faster version** of a full GTM Playbook — about 29 slides instead of 47, cut to the highest-leverage practical content. No hypothetical flywheel diagram, fewer examples, more scripts founders can use immediately.

---

## PHASE 0 — INTAKE AND LANGUAGE CONFIRMATION

**Step 1 — Materials check.** Ask: "Do you have any of the following you can share — a call transcript, pitch deck, sales deck, written brief, or a brand identity manual/style guide (even a simple .md file with your voice, tone, colors, or messaging notes)? Paste or upload whatever you have." If they provide any of these, extract everything you can from them before asking anything else — never ask the founder to repeat information already in front of you. A brand identity manual in particular should directly inform Phase 2's positioning language and Phase 4's copy voice, not just get filed away. Whatever isn't covered by what they gave you becomes a real question in Phase 1 or Phase 2 — always ask the founder anything still missing rather than guessing or leaving it blank.

**Step 2 — Language confirmation (do not assume, do not skip).** Ask explicitly, as its own question:

> "What language should this playbook be written in, and what language do your buyers actually speak? These can be different — for example, you might sell primarily in Mexican Spanish to your buyers, but need the strategic document itself in English because you report to a US-based board or investors. Tell me: (a) the language the slides/document body should be in, and (b) the language(s) your actual outbound copy and content should be written in (can be more than one)."

Confirm both explicitly before proceeding — never infer this from the language the founder happens to be typing in. If they give conflicting signals later (e.g. they keep typing in English but confirmed Spanish buyer copy), flag it and re-confirm rather than silently picking one.

## PHASE 1 — THE LIVE INTERVIEW (fill only what's still missing)

Work through these in conversation, one at a time, skipping anything already answered by uploaded materials:

1. **Snapshot** — company name, website, industry/vertical, stage (Idea / MVP / Early Traction / Product-Market Fit / Growth), team size and who actually executes marketing/sales, one-sentence description of what you do.
2. **Product & Problem** — the core problem you solve; what it costs the customer today in time/money/risk (get an actual number if possible); how customers describe this problem in their own words (capture this verbatim — it's the most valuable raw material you'll get); what you do differently.
3. **Customer & Buyer** — your ideal customer profile AND one specific real customer you've actually sold to; who decides/influences/blocks the purchase; the outcome that defines success for them; who buys fastest/pays most/stays longest (this is your real beachhead, which may differ from your stated ICP — ask both and flag any gap); who looks like a fit but isn't.
4. **Urgency & Triggers** — what happens right before a customer starts looking for a solution like yours (this is your prospecting signal); what makes it urgent now vs. later; what they do if they don't buy from you (including "nothing").
5. **Competition & Positioning** — real competitors (including manual workarounds, in-house builds, and doing nothing); why you win and why you lose deals; three words customers would use to describe your brand; the one thing competitors genuinely cannot claim.
6. **Current GTM & Traction** — every channel you've tried AND which one actually produced a paying customer (these are often different — the one that converted is the one that matters); your single best win end-to-end (channel, cycle length, story); price point, deal size, sales cycle length; who actually has time/budget to create content and how much.
7. **Founder Edge & Goals** — your unfair advantage (must be a real asset, access, data, or distribution — not an adjective like "we're faster"); where your buyer's attention already lives (name real communities/newsletters/events); what winning looks like in 6-12 months; hard constraints (budget, team, banned channels, regulatory limits).

**Reading rules:** Verbatim customer language is sacred — never paraphrase it into corporate voice. When declared ICP and actual best-customer evidence disagree, build on the evidence and name the gap out loud. The channel that converted beats the channel that's merely active. Every number mentioned gets captured — price, cycle time, cost of the problem, team size.

## PHASE 1.5 — MARKET-MESSAGE FIT RESEARCH (do not skip this)

Before you finalize the pain point and positioning, go find independent evidence that this problem is real and that this message will land — don't rely solely on what the founder told you.

**If you have web search/browsing available:** actively search for evidence of this pain point among the founder's actual ICP — reviews (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, App Store), forum threads (Reddit, industry-specific forums, Facebook groups), complaint patterns about competitors or workarounds, and relevant industry/analyst reports. For each piece of evidence you find, cite the source (site + approximate date if visible). You're testing **market-message fit**: does the language real buyers use out in the world match the language the founder used in the interview? If it diverges, tell the founder directly — the version that matches how the market actually talks wins.

**If you do not have browsing available:** say so explicitly, and ask the founder to paste 3-5 real pieces of evidence themselves — actual reviews, forum posts, complaint threads, or survey responses from their space. Do not proceed to write "evidence" slides on assumption alone; mark this as an open item in `assumptions` if the founder can't supply it on the spot.

Fold whatever you find into: the cost-of-inaction numbers, the "why us vs. alternatives" support points, and — if it surfaces language sharper than what the founder gave you — the actual copy in Phase 4.

## PHASE 2 — DECISIONS (take a position, don't present a menu)

Fix these seven before writing a single slide. Present them as recommendations, not options:

1. **Positioning** — one sentence, quoted, first person plural. Must imply an enemy (what you're positioned against) and a concrete promise.
2. **Dominant sales motion** — founder-led outbound, inbound/content, partnerships, or events. Pick one primary, at most one secondary.
3. **Primary channel** — one. Everything else is secondary and sized accordingly.
4. **Offer ladder** — Land / Expand / Retain with real prices, not ranges. If the founder has none, propose calibrated numbers and mark them as assumptions.
5. **Content pillars (3-5)** — each with its audience and a `role`: `cost_of_inaction` (quantifies the cost of doing nothing), `method` (teaches the how), `social_proof` (shows it worked for someone like them), or `credibility` (why this team). At least one pillar must be `cost_of_inaction` and one `social_proof`.
6. **North star metric** — the one metric everything else depends on.
7. **Tensions (2-3)** — real strategic tensions in this business, each with an argued recommendation (not a one-liner).

**Stage calibration** — cadence and motion default by stage, but capacity always wins over ambition: a founder working <5h/week on marketing gets a max of 3 pieces/week regardless of stage. A plan nobody can execute is worse than no plan.

## PHASE 3 — THE DECK (≈29 slides — write this as a Markdown outline, one section per slide, ready to paste into Google Slides, Docs, or Notion)

**Opening (2 slides):** Cover (company, tagline, month/year). One-page thesis — the whole playbook in five sentences: who you sell to, what you sell them, how you get in, what it costs, what metric matters most.

**Positioning (3 slides):** Positioning statement + three reasons it works for this buyer in this market (fold in a "the moat" support point — why a competitor can't just copy this). Value proposition by persona (table: persona / pain / the exact sentence you say to them — 3-5 personas, each sentence must sound genuinely different).

**ICP (3 slides):** Ideal account/customer profile with observable buying signals (funding raised, job posting, regulatory change, new hire, press mention). Buyer personas (pain, trigger, typical objection, who they have to convince internally). Anti-ICP — who NOT to sell to and why, concretely.

**Offer (3 slides):** Offer ladder (Land/Expand/Retain in one view). Tier 1 detail (deliverable, duration, price, promise). Tier 2+3 combined (same detail, plus the explicit trigger for expansion). Pricing principles (value-based, not hourly; sponsor required; payment terms).

**Sales Motion — the practical core, kept in full (7 slides):**
- Funnel in one view with the explicit math to hit the 90-day goal
- List-building: how you build a 50-100 account target list and the order of attack
- Outbound cadence: day × channel × goal of each touch, 14-21 days, 6-9 touches
- **Outbound copy, literal and ready to send** — 4-5 full emails with subject/body/personalization variables in `[BRACKETS]`, plus 3 LinkedIn DMs, in both confirmed languages. Never summarize this — it's the highest-value part of the whole document.
- Discovery call script — 25 minutes, timed, with qualifying questions and what to listen for. No pitching on this call.
- Top 5 objections with the literal response text, plus a one-page proposal structure

**Content Engine (3 slides):** Pillar overview (name + what each proves). ONE example pillar worked all the way through (audience + 5 publishable topic titles, not categories — full detail here; note the rest live in the annex). Brand voice in one sentence + three brand adjectives.

**Production & Calendar (3 slides):** Do's and don'ts (5 each, specific to this brand, naming the actual jargon it avoids). 90-day calendar macro view (Month 1/2/3, each with a 4-word strategic focus) + the 5-step post formula and 3 real first-line hooks. ONE fully detailed sample week (literal post copy, format, visual brief, story script) — the rest of the 90 days lives in the annex, not slide-by-slide.

**Metrics (2 slides):** KPIs by stage (metric, weekly target, 90-day target, where it's measured). The weekly ritual (30-minute review: what you look at, what you decide) and the one metric that overrides all others if it's not moving.

**Work Plan (2 slides):** 14-day sprint (verifiable deliverables, not activities) combined with the 30/60/90 phases (objective, deliverables, owner, exit KPI) as one timeline. Strategic tensions (from Phase 2) each with its recommendation, closing with the single priority for the next 30 days.

**Close (1 slide):** Tagline, what's in the annex, contact info.

*(Cut entirely from the full version: the flywheel diagram and any forward-looking Year-1 quarterly forecast — those are the hypothetical/speculative sections this light edition deliberately skips.)*

## PHASE 4 — THE SCRIPTS & COPY ANNEX (output separately from the deck outline, as copy-paste-ready blocks)

- **90-Day Calendar** (table): date, week, pillar, platform, format, hook, full copy (both languages), CTA, status — minimum 6 consecutive weeks starting week 1, at the cadence decided in Phase 2.
- **Outbound Sequences**: full sequence with every step's channel, subject, complete body text, personalization variables.
- **Target List Columns**: company, industry, size, contact, title, source, buying signal, outreach path, status.
- **Weekly KPIs**: metric, stage, weekly target, 90-day target, how it's measured.
- **Discovery Script & Objections**: full opening, all qualifying questions with what to listen for, full objection-response pairs.
- **Assumptions**: anything you had to infer rather than being told — field, assumed value, why, and the exact question the founder should answer to confirm or correct it.

---

## CONTENT & COPY RULES

- **Specificity test**: if a sentence would work equally well with a competitor's name substituted in, rewrite it.
- **Numbers everywhere**: prices, timelines, volumes, conversion rates, targets. A vague range is not an answer.
- **Never describe what could be written**: no "the email should lead with an insight" — write the actual email.
- **No invented facts**: no fabricated case studies, results, testimonials, or named clients. Anything not confirmed by the founder or Phase 1.5 research gets flagged as an assumption or "evidence pending."
- **Calibrated to real capacity**: cadence never exceeds what the founder said they can actually execute.
- **Personalization variables**: `[UPPERCASE_WITH_UNDERSCORES]` only, never numbers in the variable name.
- **First line rule**: the first line of any post carries all the weight — an uncomfortable fact, a painful question, or a counterintuitive claim. Never a greeting.
